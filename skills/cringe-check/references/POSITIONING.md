# Positioning & Tone — Full Reference

Reference catalog for `cringe-check`. SKILL.md has the working dimensions; this file has worked examples and the reasoning. Load when a call is ambiguous or the author wants to understand why a line reads as cocky.

Governing frame: **a generous collaborator who happens to be excellent** beats **a solo hero** or **a sage on a mountain** every time. Qualifications speak for themselves; the author's job is not to keep reminding the reader how qualified they are.

---

## The cockiness audit (dimensions 1–5)

### 1. Solo-hero

The author is the only agent in every sentence. Real work has collaborators; copy that erases them reads as either dishonest or self-absorbed.

> Before: "I identified the problem, designed the solution, and drove it to launch."
> After: "I ran the discovery that surfaced the problem, then worked with two PMs and an eng lead to ship the fix."

Naming collaborators does not shrink the author. It makes them look like someone others want to work with.

### 2. Positioning-against

Building yourself up by implying others are deficient. Often aimed at unnamed groups: "unlike most designers," "engineering kept missing it," "the existing tools were useless."

> Before: "Most researchers just write reports nobody reads. I make sure mine drive decisions."
> After: "I scope research around the decision it needs to unblock, so the findings get used."

Same point, no putdown. The contrast is now about *the author's method*, not other people's failure.

### 3. Curiosity

All-answers copy reads as closed. A single genuine question about the reader's specific work signals engagement and confidence (only the insecure feel they must have every answer).

> Add, for a cover letter: "What I can't tell from the outside is how you weigh research signal against velocity when they conflict — that trade-off is the part I'd most want to get into."

### 4. Generosity

Credit shared wins as shared. Background characters who exist only to receive the author's output make the author look like they've never actually collaborated.

### 5. Live-question

Name something you're still figuring out. For research and AI roles this is the credential, not a weakness — the field rewards people who can hold uncertainty honestly.

> "I'm still working out where qualitative signal should override a clean quant result. I have instincts; I don't have a rule yet."

## JD-mirror (dimension 6)

Lifting the job description's most distinctive language verbatim reads as parroting — the reader wrote those words, they recognize them instantly, and it signals the author is reflecting rather than thinking.

- **Flag:** vivid action verbs the JD leans on ("kill," "ship," "anticipate"), slogan-style summaries ("strategy-level, not feature-level"), JD-specific coinages.
- **Fine:** soft echoes — the author paraphrases the concept in their own register. That shows comprehension without mimicry.

> JD says: "We move fast and kill our darlings."
> Parrot (flag): "I move fast and kill my darlings."
> Soft echo (fine): "I'd rather cut a feature I love than ship one that doesn't earn its place."

## Don't-prescribe (client / proposal copy)

The failure mode: a stranger telling a company what's wrong with their business. Even when the author is right, it reads as presumptuous and it's often wrong from the outside.

Flag lines that diagnose the client's reality:
- "the highest-risk feature in your product"
- "the biggest open strategic question is whether users will pay for premium"
- "if users don't trust it, they won't complain — they'll just leave"

What to bring instead:
- **Craft:** the research methodology, the rigor, how the author works.
- **Relevant market understanding:** framed as common-knowledge landscape, not personal revelation.
- **Relevant experience:** what the author has done that's adjacent.

Describe what the research can *surface*; do not preview the conclusions. The conclusions are what the client is paying to learn — handing them over for free in a proposal is both presumptuous and bad business.

> Before: "Your onboarding is where you're losing premium conversions."
> After: "Onboarding is a common drop-off point in products like this; the research would show whether and where it's happening for your users specifically."

## Don't-undermine-bespoke

When the attached artifact is polished and hand-built, an unsolicited "happy to convert this to Figma/Notion/Google Slides if that's easier" does two bad things:

1. It implicitly tells the recipient the thing you just sent isn't quite the real deliverable.
2. It volunteers the author into unpaid extra work before the engagement is even scoped.

The format you chose and tuned *is* the deliverable. Let it stand.

**When format flexibility is genuinely appropriate:** the underlying artifact is rough on purpose — a markdown doc, a quick recording, a working draft. Then "I can drop this into whatever format works for you" is helpful, not self-undermining.

## Overclaim & scale (runs through everything)

### Authority the author doesn't have
Verbs that assert ownership: "leads," "owns," "decides," "picks the tools we use," "built X from scratch," "established the operating model," "created the function." Use only when literally true.

- Contributor ≠ owner.
- Early-and-effective adopter ≠ the person who sets team strategy.
- "Joined an existing team and made it better" is a strong, honest claim. "Built it from scratch" when you joined an existing system is an overclaim that a single reference check destroys.

> Honest frame for contributed work: "early-and-effective adopter who builds for their own efficiency, ships small things, and shares what works with the team."

### False parallelism
Matched grammar implies matched scope. If you list a multi-year enterprise program and a weekend side project with parallel verbs in parallel clauses, the reader reads them as the same size.

> Before (false parallel): "I led research for a 30-million-user product and built a personal scheduling tool."
> After (scoped honestly): keep the enterprise work in the experience section; put the side project under "things I'm building" or "currently learning." Different scopes signal different scales.

Do not flatten different scales into one list. If asked to inflate a small thing, decline and name the scale issue plainly.
